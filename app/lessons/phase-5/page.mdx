# Lesson 5: Transistor and Circuit-Level Emulation

Lessons 1-4 established field representation, observer effects, tensor transforms, and architecture-level tradeoffs.

Lesson 5 explains where those tradeoffs come from at the circuit layer.

## Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain how transistor and RC-style behavior drives architecture-level outcomes.
- Connect waveform shape to latency, drift, and stability.
- Distinguish architecture simulation from circuit-level emulation.

## Why this lesson exists

Architecture comparisons summarize behavior at a high level.

Circuit models reveal the physical causes underneath:

- Binary behavior emerges from pull-up/pull-down switching dynamics.
- Analog behavior emerges from integration, filtering, leakage, and environmental perturbation.
- Ternary behavior emerges from multi-threshold branching around balanced states.

This is the bridge between logic abstractions and electrical behavior.

## Binary CMOS lens

Model focus:

- inverter-chain switching
- pull-up/pull-down conductance competition
- propagation delay and settling

Primary question:

How quickly and cleanly does a discrete edge settle to a stable logic level?

## Analog RC lens

Model focus:

- continuous RC integration
- low-pass filtering and attenuation
- temperature/noise drift

Primary question:

How much lag and amplitude loss appears under realistic analog conditions?

## Ternary multi-threshold lens

Model focus:

- low/mid/high conduction branches
- balanced ternary thresholds around zero
- neutral-state stability versus rapid flips

Primary question:

How do threshold choices preserve symmetry while keeping transitions stable?
